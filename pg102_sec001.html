Then in 1979, Mauja at last gave birth to a baby girl. They called her Martina. Mabala realised he had to save and prepare for the future. He tried to save from his salary, but this was at the time when prices went up… and up … and up. Mama Martina tried to help him by making mats and selling them, but still, the prices went up. She started to cook chapattis to sell, but still, the prices went up. Finally, Mabala and his wife realised that this life was impossible. They were running faster and faster, but they were not moving at all. They were working harder and harder, but their life remained the same. So, one day Mabala and his wife sat down and discussed what to do. Martina was now ready to go to Standard One and they didn’t even know how they would buy her uniform. Mabala suggested that they go to the village, as life there was easier than town life. Mama Martina grew up in the village, so she told him that life on the farm was not easy. One needs to work hard to have a good life. “Rubbish,” said Mabala again, “You dig, you plant, you weed, and you eat. No problem.” Mama Martina realised her husband was in a stubborn mood. She suggested she should go to her relative in Morogoro where there was a lot of land. She would cultivate there while Mabala continued to work. If she succeeded, then Mabala could join her on the farm. Mabala agreed, so that year Mama Martina moved to her relatives in Morogoro. She got a farm of ten acres. Of course, that year, she could not cultivate the whole farm but she planted according to the instructions of the Bwana Shamba and she used fertiliser in the right way, so she got 60 bags of maize. Now, 60 bags of maize were more than Mabala’s salary for the whole year. So, they decided that Mabala would resign and come to join his wife and Martina in the village. Adapted from Mabala R. S.
