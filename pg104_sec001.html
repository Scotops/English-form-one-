DO YOU KNOW?

Retelling a story means presenting the story you have read or heard without leaving out key information or adding irrelevant information. It enables one to develop recalling, sequencing and imagination skills. Moreover, it facilitates one’s building of vocabulary and strengthening of comprehension skills. It also prepares one for successful writing, critical thinking and communication.

Here are some techniques for a successful retelling of a story.

  1. 1.

    Identify and put the main events in order. This involves knowing the flow of events (plot).

  2. 2.

    Use the “first, next, then, last” formula. In retelling the story, one needs to have a structure through which he or she will organise the story from what happened first, how the events of the story developed and what happened last as the story got to the end.

  3. 3.

    Map the story before retelling it by visualising it or understanding it so that you break it into key elements (characters, setting, plot, setting, conflict, and resolution). Mapping a story helps one to understand and remember the events.

  4. 4.

    Focus on the plot structure (elements) exposition, rising action, climax, falling action or resolution to enable you to sequence the events.

  5. 5.

    Practise retelling the story in a pair or group. Listen to each other’s story, encourage one another and learn from others. This will help you learn how to retell a story to others.

(b) Read the story about “Kalulu the Hare” and retell it to the class.
