Football frenzy

It was almost the end of the Premier League season. Fans everywhere were excited about who was going to be the champion that year. I am not a football fan, but I can always overhear discussions about football as I interact with the community around me. Two big teams, Msimbazi and Jangwani, are the most famous teams, with a huge fan base in the country. Given their huge number of supporters, it is common to hear fans arguing about the strengths of their teams in different gatherings, including markets, buses, and trains. I normally take a train, famously known as Mwakyembe, from Tabata to the city centre, where I work.

“Greetings, Msimbazi and Jangwani fans!” the train operator was heard through the speakers in one of the carriages I was in. Oh my goodness! That greeting sparked a conversation among the passengers.

“Msimbazi is not a team”, one passenger shouted. There was a loud jeer from Msimbazi fans.

“Don’t talk about our team. We have beaten you twice this year!” one Msimbazi fan responded.

“You’re trailing by six points! Just admit, will you? In this season, the trophy is ours.” Another Jangwani fan responded.

“We have beaten you so many times. We have taken the trophy four years consecutively. We can beat you this time around,” another Msimbazi fan said.

“We just need one or two games, and it will be over!”

“Buguruni passengers, get ready,” an announcement to alert the passengers on the next train stop was heard.

“My goodness! I was supposed to drop at Kwamnyamani. I missed my stop,” one of the talkative passengers said.

A burst of laughter erupted from all the passengers. They put aside their differences for that moment and laughed their way out. I, on the other hand, could not stop smiling.
