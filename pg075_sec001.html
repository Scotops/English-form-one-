Printed book page 69. DO YOU KNOW? The italicised words are called articles. Articles are words used before nouns to provide information about such nouns. They help to specify whether the noun is referring to something specific or general. There are three main articles in English: a, an and the. These articles can be grouped into two: indefinite and definite articles. 1. Indefinite articles, a and an are used before singular nouns to refer to any member of a group. An is used in the following contexts: (i) before a singular word that begins with a vowel (e.g. an apple, an egg, an order). (ii) before words beginning with silent h ( e.g. an hour, an heir). A is used before words that begin with a consonant sound. NB: The article a is also used with words that begin with a vowel which is pronounced like consonant y (e.g. a university, a European, a unit). 2. The definite article, the, is used before a singular or plural noun to indicate that the identity of the noun is known, or a noun has been mentioned earlier in the same text. The following examples are illustrative. (i) The car in the garage is mine (the is used to indicate a specific, as opposed to any other car). (ii) I saw a dog in the park. The dog was chasing a ball.
